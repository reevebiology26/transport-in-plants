PAG 5 Reminder: Using a Potometer

Independent Variable: Abiotic factor (e.g., Wind speed via fan distance, Light intensity, Temperature, or Humidity).

Dependent Variable: Rate of water uptake (mm³ min⁻¹) — used to estimate transpiration rate.

Control Variables: Same leafy shoot (constant leaf surface area & stomatal density), temperature, light intensity, humidity, air movement.

Essential OCR A Method & Precautions

  1. Cut a leafy shoot under water and at a slant (45°) to prevent air bubbles entering xylem vessels (which would break the continuous water column) and to increase surface area for water uptake.
  2. Assemble the entire potometer apparatus under water and seal all joints with waterproof petroleum jelly (Vaseline) so the system is airtight.
  3. Dry the leaves gently with a paper towel (so stomata are not blocked by water) and allow the shoot to acclimatise for 10 minutes.
  4. Introduce a single air bubble into the capillary tube and record its starting position on the millimeter scale.
  5. Measure the distance moved ($l$) by the air bubble in a fixed time ($t$, e.g. 5 minutes). Use the reservoir tap to reset the bubble and take repeats.
  6. Calculation: Volume of water taken up $= \pi r^2 l$ (where $r$ = radius of capillary bore). Rate $= \frac{\pi r^2 l}{t}$.
  7. Exam Trap: A potometer measures rate of water uptake, not transpiration directly, because a small fraction (~1–5%) of water is used in photosynthesis and to maintain cell turgidity.

PAG 1 & 2: Stained Sections & Stem Dissection

PAG 1 (Light Microscopy): Examining and drawing low-power tissue plans of stained TS/LS herbaceous dicot roots, stems, and leaves.

PAG 2 (Dissection): Cutting thin transverse (TS) and longitudinal (LS) sections of plant stems (e.g., celery or busy lizzie) using a sharp scalpel/razor blade away from fingers on a white tile.

Key Specification Details

  1. Staining: Stain sections with Toluidine Blue O (stains lignified xylem walls blue-green and cellulose phloem/parenchyma pinkish-purple) or Phloroglucinol + HCl (stains lignin red).
  2. Biological Drawing Rules: Use a sharp HB pencil, continuous single lines (no sketching/shading), draw a low-power tissue plan showing tissue boundaries (no individual cells unless high-power), include a title, scale/magnification, and horizontal ruled label lines.
  3. Root vs Stem vs Leaf Distribution:
    • Root: Central star/X-shaped xylem with phloem nestled between arms, surrounded by pericycle and endodermis.
    • Stem: Discrete vascular bundles arranged in a peripheral ring; xylem on the inside, phloem on the outside, separated by cambium.
    • Leaf: Vascular bundles form the midrib and network of veins; xylem on the upper (adaxial) side, phloem on the lower (abaxial) side.
